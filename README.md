Abstract—The continuous advancement in VLSI (Very-Large-
Scale Integration) technology necessitates robust and efficient
testing methodologies to ensure the reliability and functionality
of integrated circuits (ICs). Traditional testing techniques, while
effective, often face challenges in coping with the increasing
complexity and miniaturization of modern circuits. This paper
explores the application of machine learning (ML) in VLSI testing,
aiming to elucidate the synergy between these two domains
and demonstrate the practical benefits of ML in IC testing.
Initially, the concepts of VLSI testing and machine learning are
introduced separately, establishing a foundational understanding
of their principles and methodologies. Subsequently, the paper
delves into various ML applications in VLSI testing, including
fault detection, test pattern generation, and behavioral testing. By
reviewing mainstream examples, the paper highlights the effectiveness
of ML in enhancing test accuracy, reducing test time, and
adapting to new testing challenges. A practical implementation is
presented to demonstrate functional testing using ML, specifically
through an 8-bit even parity generator. Utilizing the PyCaret
library, multiple classifiers were evaluated to predict the parity
from 8-bit inputs. The Ridge Classifier and Linear Discriminant
Analysis both achieved an accuracy of 47.58%, while a neural
artificial neural network (ANN) outperformed other models with
an accuracy of 63.89%. These results underscore the potential of
ML techniques to improve VLSI testing processes significantly.
This paper aims to provide insights into the integration of ML in
VLSI testing, showcasing how ML can enhance the reliability and
efficiency of IC testing and paving the way for future research
and development in this interdisciplinary field.

Index Terms—VLSI Testing, Machine Learning, Fault Detection,
Test Pattern Generation, Design Verification, Parity Generator,
PyCaret, Ridge Classifier, Linear Discriminant Analysis,
Classification Accuracy, Artificial Neural Networks (ANN).
